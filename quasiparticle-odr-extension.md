## Abstract

The Ontological Distribution of Reality (ODR) framework identifies a particle with its Compton count $N_C = m/m_P \in \mathbb{Q}^+$, a dimensionless rational number whose prime factorization assigns it a fixed vertex on the Bruhat--Tits tree. This paper extends that framework to address a natural objection: what of quasiparticles -- phonons, magnons, Cooper pairs, and dressed electrons -- whose effective mass depends on the host material? We argue that the conceptual distinction between "fundamental particle" and "quasiparticle" is itself anthropocentric and scale-dependent. In the pure ontology of the BT tree, both are just rational ratios of frequencies between two background states -- the electron's Compton count is already a cross-ratio against the Planck scale, and a quasiparticle's effective Compton count is a nested cross-ratio against the host lattice. We formalize this through the cross-ratio as the fundamental coordinate of the BT tree, derive the quasiparticle Compton count as a rational function of fundamental Compton counts, and provide falsifiability conditions. The radical conclusion: particles and quasiparticles are ontologically identical in the BT tree; the distinction is purely practical (lifetime, dispersion relation), not metaphysical.

**Keywords:** Ontological Distribution of Reality, Bruhat--Tits tree, quasiparticles, cross-ratio, Compton frequency, p-adic valuation, place-democracy, measurement inseparability

## 1. Introduction

The Ontological Distribution of Reality (ODR) framework defines a particle as an invariant dimensionless rational number — its Compton count $N_C = m/m_P \in \mathbb{Q}^+$ — which assigns it a fixed coordinate on the Bruhat--Tits (BT) tree via its prime factorization [established — ODR v3.0, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21755425]. This identification recasts the question "what is a particle?" from a spatial question (where is it?) to a combinatorial one (what is its ratio?).

A natural objection arises from condensed matter physics. The particles studied in solids — electrons, phonons, magnons, Cooper pairs — have effective masses $m^*$ that depend on the host crystal, temperature, doping, and external fields. If a particle is defined by a constant Compton count, what is an entity whose Compton count changes with the environment?

This paper provides the ODR answer, which proceeds in two deepening stages. The first stage (Sections 2--4) argues that quasiparticles are rational functions of background Compton counts — not invariant vertices but context-dependent edge traversals. The second stage (Sections 5--6) pushes further: it shows that the very distinction between "fundamental particle" and "quasiparticle" dissolves under the cross-ratio ontology of the BT tree. The electron's Compton count is already a cross-ratio against the Planck scale; the measurement apparatus and the background are inseparable; every measurement in the BT tree is a cross-ratio of four vertices. The radical conclusion is that particles and quasiparticles are ontologically identical — both are just rational ratios of frequencies between two background states.

We proceed through seven sections. Section 2 reviews the Compton frequency across disciplines. Section 3 formalizes three definitions of a "particle." Section 4 resolves the priority question: is a particle more invariant than the CMB? Section 5 presents the cross-ratio argument — the missing half of the thesis. Section 6 formalizes the quasiparticle as a nested cross-ratio with falsifiability conditions. Section 7 discusses implications.

## 2. The Compton Frequency Across Disciplines

### 2.1 Quantum Field Theory: Intrinsic Oscillation

In quantum field theory, the Compton frequency is the intrinsic rest-frame oscillation of a free particle. For a particle of mass $m$, the angular frequency is:

$$\omega_C = \frac{m c^2}{\hbar}$$

In natural units ($\hbar = c = 1$), this reduces to $\omega_C = m$. The Compton frequency serves as the "clock rate" for quantum phase evolution [established — standard QFT]. For the electron, the cyclic Compton frequency is approximately $1.236 \times 10^{20}$ Hz.

Historically, the Dirac equation's Zitterbewegung (trembling motion) was associated with frequency $2mc^2/\hbar$. However, this is understood in modern QFT as an interference artifact between positive- and negative-energy solutions that vanishes under second quantization — it is not a physical oscillation of the particle [established — standard QFT interpretation].

### 2.2 Condensed Matter: A Probe, Not a Parameter

In condensed matter physics, the term "Compton frequency" is rarely used as an isolated parameter. The focus is on **Compton scattering**: the inelastic scattering of X-rays or gamma rays off electrons to probe their ground-state momentum density. The central observable is the **Compton profile** — the spectral line shape encoding the electron momentum distribution [established].

Research emphasis falls on inhomogeneous systems (liquid metals), strong electron correlation effects, and high magnetic field influences on the Compton profile.

### 2.3 The ODR Bridge

ODR's contribution is to elevate the Compton frequency from a "property that a particle happens to have" to "what a particle is." The dimensionless Compton count:

$$N_C = \frac{m}{m_P} \in \mathbb{Q}^+$$

is the particle's identity. Its prime factorization determines its depth and position on the BT tree. All other properties (energy, wavelength, interaction strengths) are functions of this root number [established — ODR v3.0].

But here a deeper question already lurks. The Planck mass $m_P = \sqrt{\hbar c / G}$ is itself defined using $G$, $\hbar$, $c$ — constants measured against our laboratory apparatus (SI kilograms, meters, seconds). Therefore, even a "fundamental" electron's $N_C \approx 4.185 \times 10^{-23}$ is **already a cross-ratio** between the electron's oscillation and the Planck scale — which is itself a background defined by our measurement system. We return to this insight in Section 5.

## 3. What Is a Particle? Three Frameworks

### 3.1 Standard QFT: Field Excitation

In quantum field theory, a particle is a quantum excitation of an underlying field — a localized ripple in the electron field, photon field, or quark field [established]. It is defined by a set of conserved quantum numbers (mass, spin, charge) and is not a persistent "thing" in the classical sense: particle number is not conserved, and particles are created and annihilated in interactions.

### 3.2 Condensed Matter: Quasiparticle as Emergent Excitation

In condensed matter, the term "particle" takes on a pragmatic meaning. **Indivisible particles** (electrons, photons) exist but are "dressed" — an electron moving through a crystal drags a cloud of virtual phonons and other electrons, acquiring an effective mass $m^*$ that can differ by orders of magnitude from the bare mass. For example, in GaAs, $m^* \approx 0.067 \times m_e$ [established].

**Quasiparticles** (phonons, magnons, electron holes) are collective excitations of many-body systems. They behave mathematically like elementary particles — carrying momentum, spin, and obeying quantum statistics — but are emergent: they do not exist outside the bulk material.

### 3.3 ODR / Bruhat--Tits Tree: Invariant Rational Number

In the ODR framework, a particle is a persistent ratio — a combinatorial coordinate on the BT tree, stripped of spatial extension, existing only as a prime-factorized relationship to the Planck scale [speculative — ODR v3.0].

The particle's identity is given entirely by its Compton count $N_C$. In the BT tree, this corresponds to a specific depth determined by the $p$-adic valuations of $N_C$. The electron is not a "thing" that has mass $m_e$; it is the ratio $N_C \approx 4.185 \times 10^{-23}$. Since the BT tree has no absolute origin, a particle is purely a relational node — defined only by how its cycle-count compares to other cycle-counts.

## 4. Particle vs. CMB: A Question of Ontological Priority

### 4.1 Standard Physics: Particles Precede States

The cosmic microwave background (CMB) is a state of the photon field — a bath of approximately $4 \times 10^{84}$ photons redshifted to a temperature of approximately 2.725 K [established]. In QFT, the underlying photon field is ontologically prior to any specific thermodynamic configuration. The CMB cannot exist without photons; therefore, the particle (field excitation) is more invariant than the state.

### 4.2 Frequency Stability

A single massive particle (such as an electron) has a strictly constant Compton frequency $\omega_C = m$. The CMB, in contrast, is a thermal blackbody spectrum — a continuous distribution of photon frequencies spanning from radio to infrared. Its "frequency" is a statistical average (the Wien peak in frequency is approximately 160 GHz at 2.725 K), not a sharp eigenfrequency [established].

Thus, even in standard physics, the particle has a precise, unchanging "note" while the CMB is a symphony of many notes.

### 4.3 ODR: The Coordinate Precedes the Cloud

In the BT tree, a particle is a single rational point — a specific depth defined by its prime factorization. The CMB is a thermodynamic ensemble: a Gibbs distribution over many possible rational coordinates (many different photon Compton counts) [speculative].

A coordinate system (the BT tree) is logically prior to a probability distribution over that system. The single, precise rational ratio is the primary object; the thermal bath is a derived, statistical artifact.

Furthermore, the CMB introduces an **Archimedean bias**. It has a specific temperature (2.725 K), which relies on the real-number continuum and thermal equilibrium. An isolated particle, with its exact Compton count, is purely combinatorial and place-democratic — its ratio is valid at every completion of $\mathbb{Q}$, not just the Archimedean one. In dimensionless Planck units (per qnfo-core §0.7: $\hbar = c = G = k_B = 1$), $T = k_B T_{\text{phys}}/E_P \approx 1.92 \times 10^{-32}$ — itself a Compton count, but one that only makes sense as a Gibbs parameter, not as an invariant vertex.

## 5. The Cross-Ratio Argument: Why the Distinction Dissolves

The preceding sections distinguished "bare particles" (invariant vertices) from "quasiparticles" (context-dependent rational functions). This is the halfway position. The cross-ratio argument, derived from the geometry of the BT tree, forces the next logical step.

### 5.1 The BT Tree Has No Privileged Origin

Cartesian space assumes an absolute origin $(0, 0, 0)$ and a fixed speed of light $c$. That requires a privileged vacuum — a background that is everywhere the same and everywhere at rest. But there is no perfect vacuum [established]. Even the QED vacuum is not empty: it is a sea of virtual particles, zero-point fluctuations, and non-zero vacuum expectation values.

The Bruhat--Tits tree has **no privileged origin**. It is an infinite, regular tree where every vertex is equivalent under the automorphism group. You never measure an absolute coordinate on the BT tree — you only measure a **cross-ratio** of four vertices [established — Kaletha & Prasad 2023]:

$$[a, b; c, d] = \frac{d_{\text{BT}}(a,c) \cdot d_{\text{BT}}(b,d)}{d_{\text{BT}}(a,d) \cdot d_{\text{BT}}(b,c)}$$

where $d_{\text{BT}}$ is the ultrametric distance on the tree.

### 5.2 The Electron's Compton Count Is Already a Cross-Ratio

The fundamental particle's Compton count $N_C = \omega / \omega_P$ appears to be an absolute ratio. But what is $\omega_P$? The Planck frequency is defined using $G$, $\hbar$, $c$, which are themselves measured against our laboratory apparatus.

Therefore, even $N_C = \omega / \omega_P$ is **already a cross-ratio** between the particle and a reference background — the Planck scale, as accessed through our measurement system. The paper's admission in the ODR Thesis (Section 2.3) that "the real number expansion is an Archimedean artifact" acknowledges this implicitly: the Planck scale is not a "thing" but a boundary in measurement space.

### 5.3 The Measurement Apparatus and the Background Are Inseparable

Every physical measurement involves four elements, which map to the four vertices of a cross-ratio:

- Vertex $a$: your particle's frequency ($\omega_{\text{particle}}$)
- Vertex $b$: the Planck frequency ($\omega_{\text{Planck}}$)
- Vertex $c$: your measurement apparatus's internal clock (e.g., a cesium atom)
- Vertex $d$: the vacuum background (zero-point fluctuations)

The measurement result is the cross-ratio $[a, b; c, d]$. You cannot disentangle the particle from the apparatus, nor the apparatus from the background. The ontology is purely relational.

### 5.4 Fundamental Particle and Quasiparticle Are Just Different Cross-Ratios

A quasiparticle does not have a fixed $N_C$ against the Planck scale. Instead, it has a fixed $N_C^{\text{eff}}$ against the **host substrate** (the lattice, the Fermi sea, or the background field):

- **Fundamental particle:** $N_C = \omega_{\text{particle}} / \omega_{\text{Planck}}$
- **Quasiparticle:** $N_C^{\text{qp}} = \omega_{\text{excitation}} / \omega_{\text{lattice}}$

But the lattice itself is made of fundamental particles (ions, electrons) which have their own Compton counts against the Planck scale. So the quasiparticle's mass is a **nested cross-ratio**:

$$N_C^{\text{qp}} = \frac{\omega_{\text{excitation}}}{\omega_{\text{lattice}}} = \frac{\omega_{\text{excitation}} / \omega_{\text{Planck}}}{\omega_{\text{lattice}} / \omega_{\text{Planck}}} = \frac{N_C^{\text{excitation}}}{N_C^{\text{lattice}}}$$

It is a rational function of two fundamental Compton counts. Both the fundamental particle and the quasiparticle are, in the end, cross-ratios.

### 5.5 The Radical Conclusion

In the pure BT tree ontology, **there is no fundamental ontological difference between an electron in free space and a phonon in a crystal.**

- Both are persistent excitations relative to a background.
- The electron's background is the QED vacuum (which is not empty — it has virtual particles).
- The phonon's background is the atomic lattice.

Both backgrounds are just collections of other frequencies. In the BT tree, a "particle" is just a stable node, and a "quasiparticle" is just a less stable node — both are defined by their prime-factorized ratio to the nodes they interact with.

The claim that the electron is more fundamental than the CMB is therefore **a concession to human habit**, not a strict consequence of the ontology. In the pure BT framework, the CMB, the electron, and the phonon are all **just coordinates on the same tree**, distinguished only by their location and the background we choose as our reference vertex.

The particle is not fundamental. **The cross-ratio between any two frequencies — whether particle-to-Planck or quasiparticle-to-lattice — is fundamental.** The BT tree does not care if you are measuring an electron or a hole. It only cares about the prime factorization of the ratio between the thing you are probing and the thing you are probing with. You cannot separate the two.

## 6. Quasiparticles as Nested Cross-Ratios

### 6.1 Formal Definition

**Definition.** A fundamental particle is a cross-ratio against the Planck scale:

$$N_C^{\text{fundamental}} = \frac{\omega_{\text{particle}}}{\omega_{\text{Planck}}}$$

A quasiparticle is a nested cross-ratio:

$$N_C^{\text{qp}}(\alpha) = \frac{\omega_{\text{excitation}}}{\omega_{\text{lattice}}} = \frac{N_C^{\text{bare}}(\alpha)}{N_C^{\text{lattice}}}$$

where $N_C^{\text{lattice}}$ is the Compton count of the collective background (lattice ions, phonon modes, Fermi sea), and $\alpha$ denotes material parameters (lattice constant, temperature, doping, external fields).

A bare particle corresponds to the special case where the background is the Planck scale itself — but since the Planck scale is operationally defined through our measurement apparatus, even this "bare" particle is a cross-ratio.

### 6.2 Geometric Interpretation on the BT Tree

**All particles (fundamental and quasiparticle):** are coordinates on the same BT tree. The difference is purely in which mode serves as the reference vertex:

- Fundamental particle: reference vertex is the Planck frequency.
- Quasiparticle: reference vertex is the lattice frequency.

On the tree, both are just pairs of vertices. The "invariance" of the fundamental particle is a statement about the stability of the ratio $\omega_{\text{electron}} / \omega_{\text{Planck}}$. The "context-dependence" of the quasiparticle is a statement about the variability of $\omega_{\text{excitation}} / \omega_{\text{lattice}}$ as the lattice changes. But both are cross-ratios — the metric that characterizes existence in any geometry without an absolute origin.

**CMB photon:** A single coordinate sampled from a Gibbs distribution over photon Compton counts. Each individual CMB photon has a definite Compton count, but the ensemble is characterized by the temperature parameter, which is an Archimedean statistic, not a combinatorial invariant.

### 6.3 Worked Example: Phonon Dispersion

Consider a monatomic linear chain with lattice constant $a$ and spring constant $K$. The phonon dispersion is:

$$\omega(q) = 2\sqrt{\frac{K}{m}} \left|\sin\left(\frac{qa}{2}\right)\right|$$

The "effective mass" of the phonon depends on the frequency (or wavevector): $m^*(\omega) \propto 1/\omega$. Its Compton count:

$$N_C^{\text{phonon}}(q) = \frac{\hbar \omega(q)}{m_P c^2}$$

is a function of the lattice parameters $(a, K, m)$ and the wavevector $q$. The 2-adic valuation $\text{ord}_2(N_C^{\text{phonon}})$ is not fixed — it varies with $q$. The phonon is a path on the BT tree, not a fixed vertex.

Under the nested cross-ratio interpretation, this is restated:

$$N_C^{\text{phonon}}(q) = \frac{N_C^{\text{excitation}}(q)}{N_C^{\text{lattice}}}$$

where $N_C^{\text{lattice}}$ is the Compton count of the unit cell (the combined mass of the ions scaled to $m_P$). The phonon's "ontological status" is identical to the electron's — both are cross-ratios. The phonon's ratio simply involves a different reference vertex.

When a zone-center phonon ($\Gamma$-point) decays into two zone-boundary phonons (K-point) via anharmonic coupling, the single rational function $N_C^{\Gamma}(0)$ decomposes into a product of two lower-frequency functions $N_C^K(q) \cdot N_C^K(-q)$. Geometrically, one cross-ratio bifurcates into two — the same process that would occur if a "fundamental" particle decayed.

### 6.4 Distinction from Bare-Dressed Renormalization

There is a superficial resemblance between "quasiparticle effective mass" and "dressed particle mass in QFT." Both involve renormalization. The distinction is:

- **QFT renormalization:** The bare mass is a UV-divergent parameter; the dressed (physical) mass is what we measure. Both exist within the same underlying field theory.
- **ODR treatment:** The bare Compton count is a cross-ratio against the Planck scale. The quasiparticle Compton count is a nested cross-ratio against the lattice scale. Both are of the same ontological kind — cross-ratios. The distinction is not between "real" and "derived" but between "reference scale A" and "reference scale B" [speculative].

### 6.5 Falsifiability

The characterization of quasiparticles as nested cross-ratios (and the stronger claim that particles and quasiparticles are ontologically identical) would be disconfirmed if:

1. **A quasiparticle were found whose effective mass $m^*$ is independent of the host material, temperature, and external fields** — i.e., if $m^*$ were a universal constant. This has not been observed; the whole of condensed matter physics is organized around the material-dependence of effective masses [established].

2. **A phonon mode were found with a Compton count whose p-adic valuations are invariant under changes to the lattice constant** — i.e., if $\text{ord}_p(N_C^{\text{phonon}})$ were constant across all materials. This is testable via DFT calculations of phonon dispersions across different crystal structures.

3. **A quasiparticle were discovered that survives the removal of its host medium** — persisting as a free excitation after the lattice is dissolved. This would contradict the emergent character of all known quasiparticles.

4. **An experiment were performed that isolates a particle's Compton count without reference to any measurement apparatus or background** — demonstrating that the cross-ratio is avoidable. This would falsify the stronger claim of ontological inseparability. No such experiment exists; the measurement problem in quantum mechanics (the inseparability of observer and observed) provides indirect support for the claim [established].

5. **The nested cross-ratio structure fails for any composite system** — if a quasiparticle's Compton count could not be expressed as a rational function of fundamental Compton counts. This would be tested by computing $\omega_{\text{excitation}} / \omega_{\text{lattice}}$ for a wide class of materials and verifying that the result is expressible as a rational combination of the constituent particles' $N_C^{\text{fundamental}}$.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 The Dissolution of the Substance-Vs-Relation Debate

The ODR treatment of quasiparticles via the cross-ratio recasts the ancient ontological debate — substance vs. relation — in geometric terms, then dissolves it:

- **v1 position:** Invariant particle = substance (fixed BT-tree vertex); quasiparticle = relation (edge traversal).
- **v2 position (this paper):** The invariant particle itself is a relation — a cross-ratio against the Planck scale. There are no substances, only cross-ratios at different scales.

This is not dualism resolving into monism; it is dualism resolving into a geometric fact: the BT tree has no origin, so every coordinate is a ratio of two reference points.

### 7.2 Anderson's "More Is Different" Reframed

Anderson's 1972 dictum that emergent phenomena are "as real as" invariant ones is recovered in ODR without contradiction, and in fact strengthened. A phonon is a real node on the BT tree — it is ontologically identical to an electron. "More is different" can be reformulated as: the nested cross-ratio structure of compound systems produces rational functions whose prime factorization involves more primes than any individual constituent, and these additional valuations are irreducible at the Archimedean completion.

### 7.3 Limitation: Finite Lifetime

A challenge not fully resolved: quasiparticles have finite lifetimes (linewidth $\Gamma$). The BT-tree formalism may need a complex-valued Compton count ($N_C + i\Gamma/m_P c^2$) to capture decay, analogous to the complex mass pole in scattering theory. This is left for future work.

### 7.4 Frontier Question

Is there a maximum "ontological distance" from a reference vertex beyond which an emergent collective excitation can no longer be approximated as a quasiparticle with a well-defined $N_C^*(\alpha)$? This would correspond to a depth threshold on the BT tree where the rational function approximation's error exceeds the intrinsic width. The nested cross-ratio framework suggests an answer: the rational function approximation breaks down when the lattice background itself loses its stable prime factorization — i.e., when the host material is disordered or strongly correlated beyond the quasiparticle paradigm.

## 8. Conclusion

The ODR framework's identification of a particle with its Compton count $N_C \in \mathbb{Q}^+$ extends naturally — not awkwardly — to condensed matter. This paper has advanced the analysis in two stages:

**Stage 1 (v1 position).** A quasiparticle is a rational function of background Compton counts:

$$N_C^*(\alpha) = f(N_C^{\text{bare}}, N_C^{\text{lattice}}, N_C^{\text{temperature}}, \dots)$$

This formalizes the condensed matter physicist's intuitive distinction between "elementary" and "emergent" excitations within ODR's geometric ontology.

**Stage 2 (v2 position — this paper).** The distinction between "fundamental particle" and "quasiparticle" is itself a scale-dependent projection of a single geometric fact: every coordinate on the BT tree is a cross-ratio. The electron's Compton count is a cross-ratio against the Planck reference; the phonon's Compton count is a nested cross-ratio against the lattice reference. Both are ontologically identical — rational ratios of frequencies between two vertices on the same tree.

The BT tree has no origin. Every coordinate is relational. The particle is not fundamental — the cross-ratio is. Quasiparticles are exactly the same ontological category as "fundamental" particles. The distinction is purely practical (lifetime, dispersion relation), not metaphysical. The BT tree absorbs both without prejudice.

The framework makes a falsifiable prediction: no experiment will ever isolate a particle's Compton count without involving a reference background and a measurement apparatus — i.e., without constructing a cross-ratio. This prediction is consistent with the measurement problem in quantum mechanics and with the entire edifice of condensed matter physics.
